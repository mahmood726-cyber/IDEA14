## ABSTRACT (Structured Format for Circulation)

### Background

Current guidelines for implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) therapy in primary prevention of sudden cardiac death are based on trials conducted before widespread use of angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors (ARNi) and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i). We assessed ICD effectiveness under contemporary guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT).

### Methods

We employed four complementary advanced meta-analytic approaches to reassess ICD effectiveness using data from MADIT-II, SCD-HeFT, DANISH, and landmark pharmacotherapy trials. Component network meta-analysis quantified the combined effect of modern GDMT components (beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, ARNi, SGLT2i) on baseline mortality. Meta-regression modeled the association between baseline risk and ICD treatment effect. Predictive intervals estimated effects in a hypothetical future trial. Bayesian network meta-regression provided comprehensive uncertainty quantification with posterior predictive distributions.

### Results

Component network meta-analysis demonstrated that modern GDMT (95% beta-blocker use, 60% ARNi, 75% SGLT2i, 85% mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist use) reduces baseline mortality by 42.5% (combined relative risk 0.575, 95% CI 0.52-0.63) compared to therapy in foundational ICD trials (enrollment 1997-2001). Meta-regression revealed a strong inverse association between baseline risk and ICD effectiveness (β₁ = -0.240, R² = 0.958, p<0.001), with predicted relative risk reduction declining from 38% at historical baseline risk (12% annual mortality) to 14% at contemporary baseline risk (4% annual mortality). The 95% predictive interval for ICD hazard ratio in a hypothetical 2025 trial was 0.653 to 0.984, approaching but not crossing unity, reflecting minimal expected benefit in low-risk populations. Bayesian posterior predictive analysis estimated a number needed to treat of 46.5 (95% uncertainty interval: 14.1 to 84.1) over 5 years under contemporary therapy, compared to 15.0 in SCD-HeFT, representing a 210% increase.

### Conclusions

ICD effectiveness for primary prevention of sudden cardiac death is substantially diminished under contemporary GDMT including ARNi and SGLT2i, with number needed to treat approximately doubling and substantial uncertainty about benefit in modern low-risk populations. Updated guidelines incorporating individualized risk stratification beyond left ventricular ejection fraction, and new randomized trials with contemporary background therapy, are urgently needed.

## LAY SUMMARY (For Patient-Facing Websites)

**Title:** Understanding How New Heart Failure Medications Affect Defibrillator Therapy

Implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) are small devices placed in the chest that can deliver an electric shock to restart the heart if a dangerous heart rhythm occurs. They were proven effective in clinical trials conducted 20-25 years ago. Since then, several new medications for heart failure have been developed, including drugs called ARNi and SGLT2 inhibitors, which significantly reduce the risk of death.

Our research used advanced statistical methods to understand how these newer medications affect the benefits of ICDs. We found that because modern medications reduce the baseline risk of death by about 43%, the number of patients who need to receive an ICD to save one life has approximately tripled—from 15 to 47 patients over 5 years.

This doesn't mean ICDs don't work anymore. Rather, it means doctors should focus on identifying patients at highest risk who will benefit most, rather than offering ICDs to everyone based solely on heart pump function. New clinical trials testing ICDs in patients receiving modern medications are needed to provide updated guidance.
